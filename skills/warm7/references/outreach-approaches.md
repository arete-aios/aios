# Five approaches and eight openers

Loaded when drafting an actual contact. Part of [WARM7](../SKILL.md).

Pick by what fits the person, not by what fits the owner's mood. Every template below is scaffolding to be rewritten in the owner's voice, never text to send as it stands.

## Five approaches

### 1 · Problem, solution, outcome

For someone whose pain is already visible.

> Hi, I am [name]. Many [audience] struggle with [pain point]. I provide [service], which [how it solves it]. That means you can [outcome], and ultimately [benefit].

*Many entrepreneurs struggle to keep any balance while scaling. I run personalised coaching that integrates mindfulness practice, so you get your output back without giving up your evenings.*

### 2 · Value proposition, audience, transformation

For someone who needs to know what makes this different.

> Hi, I am [name]. I specialise in [USP], helping [audience] who [situation]. With [service] you will be able to [outcome], resulting in [benefit].

### 3 · Storytelling

For someone who will recognise themselves in a picture faster than in a description. The strongest opener with people who do not think of themselves as having a "problem".

> Hi, I am [name]. Imagine [situation the audience knows]. That is where I come in. I help [audience] with [service], so that [outcome]. Unlike others, [USP], which means [benefit].

### 4 · Vision

For peers, partners and people who care what the owner is building rather than what they sell.

> Hi, I am [name]. My mission is [vision]. I work with [audience] to help them [outcome] through [service]. By [USP], you can [benefit].

### 5 · Expertise and guarantee

For someone who needs the risk taken off the table before they will move.

> Hi, I am [name], and I work in [field]. I help [audience] achieve [outcome] through [service]. What sets me apart is [USP], and I back it with [guarantee], so you [benefit].

## Eight openers that are easy to say yes to

Each asks for something small and gives something first. Adapt the wording. Never send eight of these to eight people in one sitting.

**Ask for feedback on an idea.**
> I am working on something new for [audience] and I would value your take on a couple of ideas. Would you be open to fifteen minutes?

**Ask permission to explain it.**
> I have launched [brief description]. I think it could be useful for [type of person they might know]. Would you be open to a short call so I can show you and you can tell me whether it fits anyone you know?

**Ask for a specific introduction.**
> I saw you are connected to [person] at [company]. I have built something that could help them with [specific issue]. Would you be willing to introduce us?

**Offer a free resource.**
> I made something for [audience] dealing with [pain point], and I am giving it away right now to get feedback. Want a look?

**Lead with the guarantee.**
> I am confident enough in [service] that I offer a full money back guarantee. Worth trying risk free?

**Bring them into the process.**
> I am building [thing], and part of that is getting input from people I trust. Could we talk briefly, and is there anything I can help you with while we are at it?

**Give something first, plainly.**
> I am giving [gift or offer] to the people who have supported me. I would like to extend it to you.

**Propose a collaboration.**
> Your work in [area] fits what I am building. Do you have time to look at whether there is something here for both of us?

## Rules that override every template

**Lead with what you can give.** Help, an introduction, a resource, honest feedback. Then ask. The order is not politeness, it is what makes the ask land.

**Be willing to ask for help.** People respond to someone being direct about needing something far better than to a soft pitch pretending not to be one. Vulnerability is a technique that only works when it is not a technique.

**Say thank you, specifically.** Not "thanks for your time". Thanks for the thing they actually did.

**Cut the closing line.** Almost every draft ends with a sentence that exists to move things toward business. Delete it. It will come up, and sooner.

**Short.** Nobody who already knows the owner needs three paragraphs of context.

**One at a time.** Fifty messages generated in one pass share a rhythm, and people who know each other compare notes. The moment two of them notice, the owner is the person who automated their friends.
