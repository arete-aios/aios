# Growing the network, not just working it

Loaded when the question becomes how the network grows, not how it gets contacted. Part of [WARM7](../SKILL.md).

Three techniques. None of them books a call this week. All three decide what the list looks like in a year.

## 1 · Imagine the network you want, then compare

Before sorting a single name, have the owner describe the network they want to have. Roughly a hundred people, with three things true of each one:

- the owner understands what that person is working toward
- the owner looks for ways to help them without expecting a return
- that person understands the owner's work well enough to send someone to them

Then put that picture beside the list that actually exists, and mark the gap. Usually most of the list fails the third test, sometimes even the TOP1 rows.

The gap is the work plan. It also reframes the job: the task is not collecting contacts, it is training a network. Someone who cannot describe the owner's work is not a weak contact, they are an untrained one, and that is fixable in one conversation.

Run this once at the start, then once a year.

## 2 · Curiosity conversations

A standing habit of talking to people with one open question that has nothing to do with work. The usual form: **what are you excited about right now.**

Give it a shape or it stays a good intention:

- **How often.** A fixed slot. Two a week is plenty. Weekly is a habit, occasional is a memory.
- **Who.** Mostly TOP3, the tier with no next step. This is the technique that keeps the bottom of the list alive without converting it into a funnel.
- **How to open.** Say what it is: *no agenda, I just want to hear what you are working on.* Naming the absence of an agenda is what makes it true.
- **How long.** Twenty to thirty minutes. Short enough that nobody has to prepare.
- **How to end.** Thank them for something specific, and stop. **No ask, no next step, no soft pitch.** If they ask what the owner is doing, answer plainly and let it sit there.

Log what they said, in their words, and nothing else. A curiosity conversation with a follow up task attached was a sales call in a costume, and the other person notices the second time.

Most of the surprising opportunities in a network arrive through these, not through the ranked and reasoned contacts. That is not a paradox. The useful information is the kind nobody knew to ask for.

## 3 · Hosting

The person who introduces two people who should know each other holds the most valuable position in any network. Hosting is the cheapest way to occupy that position on purpose.

Practical shape:

- **Small.** Six to ten people. Larger and everyone talks to whoever they arrived with.
- **Mixed.** People from different parts of the list who would not otherwise meet. That mixing is the whole product.
- **A reason to gather that is not networking.** A dinner, a walk, a run, a book, a question worth arguing about. Anything billed as networking attracts people who want something and repels everyone else.
- **The host's job is introductions, not entertainment.** Two sentences per pair: who each one is, why these two should talk. Then step away and let it run.

Do not invite anyone in order to sell to them in the room. The owner's return is being the person who made the room exist. It arrives later, and it arrives larger.
