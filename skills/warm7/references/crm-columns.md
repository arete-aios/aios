# The list: columns and tiers

Loaded when you are building or maintaining the contact list. Part of [WARM7](../SKILL.md).

## Columns that earn their place

A spreadsheet is enough. What matters is that it is one place.

| Column | Why it is there |
|---|---|
| **First name** | Separate from the surname, so personalisation never produces "Hi John Smith" |
| **Last name** | Keeps the tone right where the relationship is formal |
| **Last interaction** (date) | The only honest measure of how warm this actually is |
| **Next interaction** (date) | **Only for TOP1 and TOP2.** Leaving it blank for TOP3 is the point, not an omission |
| **Phone** | With country code |
| **Email** | Skip if the relationship lives on another platform |
| **Relationship** | How they know each other. Restores the context in one glance a year later |
| **Platform** | Where conversation actually happens with this person. Contacting someone where they do not live gets silence that reads as rejection |
| **URL** | Their profile or site |
| **Industry or role** | Lets the approach fit the person |
| **Where I can help them** | The most valuable column and the one most people leave empty. Fill it first |
| **What they could bring** | Referrals, introductions, collaboration, a client. Be specific, and accept "nothing, they are a friend" as a complete answer |
| **Notes** | What was said, in their words. Quote rather than summarise |

## The three tiers

Rank on two axes at once: **how likely business flows in either direction**, and **how warm the relationship actually is**. Someone with no need for the service but a wide network is TOP1, because referral is the point.

**TOP1.** Strong relationship and a valuable network. Contact first. They get a next date and three written reasons to talk.

**TOP2.** Real value but more work to activate. Good friends outside the industry, professionals whose relevance is indirect. They get a next date, further out.

**TOP3.** Not a business prospect and may never be. Worth keeping, worth caring about, **no next date.** Most people belong here and that is a healthy list, not a weak one.

**Tiers move.** Someone changes job and becomes TOP1 overnight. A TOP1 goes quiet for a year and drops. Re-rank when something changes, not on a schedule.

## Clusters to work through when collecting

Prompt by cluster rather than asking for a list. Nobody can answer "who do you know". Almost everybody can answer "who did you work with three jobs ago".

- **Family, including extended.** Aunts, cousins, in-laws. Ask parents and siblings who they know
- **Friends**, including the ones with no connection to the work. They know people
- **Current and former colleagues**, going back as far as memory reaches
- **Past clients**, including the ones from years ago. Dormant does not mean cold
- **Suppliers and service providers.** The accountant, the web developer, the photographer
- **Courses, communities, associations, alumni**
- **Neighbours, parents from school, sports and hobby groups**
- **Everyone in the phone.** The export always surfaces names memory does not

**Do not filter while collecting.** The ranking happens afterwards and it happens badly if it happens during.

## Train the list

Once the list exists, the highest return question is not "who can buy from me". It is **does this person know what I actually do.**

Most will not, precisely. Someone who cannot describe it cannot refer it, and that gap is itself a reason to make contact: *let me tell you what I actually do now.* That message is easy to send, easy to receive, and it is how a network turns into a referral source.

The same applies in reverse, and it comes first. **Learn what they do and how you could refer to them.** A list where the owner can introduce two people to each other is worth more than a list where they can only ask.
