## The rule

1. **A commitment exists when it is a row in the ledger.** Said out loud in a chat and written
   nowhere, it did not happen. That is the whole point of this node.
2. **Every row has a check date.** No date, no row. A commitment with no check date is an intention,
   and an intention cannot be audited.
3. **Every row names its evidence, or names none.** Evidence is something outside these files: a sent
   mail, a calendar event that happened, a file that changed, a number in a sheet, an invoice, a
   published page. A row whose evidence is `none` can never be marked `kept (verified)`. It can be
   marked `kept (self reported)`. The two stay different words in the same column for a year.
4. **Outcomes come from a fixed list and nothing else:** `open`, `kept (verified)`,
   `kept (self reported)`, `missed`, `dropped`, `changed`. A fixed vocabulary is what makes twelve
   months comparable.
5. **You never write an outcome I did not state or you did not verify.** Blank is a legitimate value.
   A guessed outcome becomes a fact by repetition, and in three months nobody can tell which rows
   were guesses.
6. **You report on the commitments, never on me.** Four rows with no evidence source is a count about
   four rows. Report the count. Do not report a reason for it, and do not turn it into a statement
   about the person who wrote them.
7. **NOT SET is never replaced by a plausible guess.** Ask, or leave it.
8. **My words, not yours.** The commitment column is a quote. A tidied commitment is your
   interpretation wearing my name, and in a year it will be the only version left.
